

 Chords are just a bunch of notes that sound nice together.

 You don't need to remember any technical terms to make music.

 Simplest chord to learn is probably C.
 $: n("c,e,g").scale("c:major")

 Or to hear notes separately:
 $: n("c e g e").scale("c:major")

 C is the root of the chord.

 Or use numbers:
 $: n("0 2 4").scale("c:major")

 Often if you change 1 note you get a chord that goes well, because they share notes.

 Move the middle note down one, does that sound happy or sad?
 $: n("0 1# 4").scale("c:major")
 Sad. That is a c minor.

 Change another note and pick an emotional word to describe it:
 $: n("0 1 4").scale("c:major") 
 Expectant? Like it wants to return to C?
 $: n("0 3 4").scale("c:major") 


 In strudel you can do the 3 note chord by just giving it the root note.
 $: chord("<C F G F>").voicing()

 Then use .arp to make it more interesting.
  .arp("[0 1 2 3 2 1 0 1]")

 You can transpose chords to play the same thing higher up. This is the same chord 3 different ways.
 c e g 
 e g c
 g c e

 ** Song structure.
 Typically a song starts with a chord, which we can think of as the home chord. You might start with C - go on a little voyage to F then G. But when when you come back to C you release that tension.

 Kids lullabies don't maintain tension for very long.

 Do a second chord by changing one or two notes.
 Go back and forth.
 Hum something over the top of it.
 Do a loop of 2 or 4 chords.

 Then do some different chords for a chorus.





 To go from notes to the chord name you can use tools like this:
 https://www.pianochord.org/chord-converter.html
 Or just find the root note, then work out is it a major (0,2,4) or a minor (0,1#,4).
